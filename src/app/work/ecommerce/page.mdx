## Overview

Ecommerce projects fail in the back office more often than on the homepage. Anyone can spin up a storefront in an afternoon; the hard part is what happens after the order: inventory, fulfillment, returns, taxes, and keeping the store in sync with the systems that actually run the business.

That intersection, where the storefront meets the ERP, the accounting system, and the warehouse, is where I have spent most of my career. The stores I build treat the storefront and the back office as one system.

The storefront side still matters, and it gets full attention: fast pages, clean product presentation, and a checkout with as little friction as the law allows. But the wins that compound are behind the scenes, where every order flows to fulfillment without a human copying it anywhere.

Whether you need a new store, a rescue of an existing one, or custom functionality your platform cannot do out of the box, the approach is the same: start from how your business actually operates and make the store serve it.
